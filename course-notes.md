- All programming languages follow the  R. D. Tennent's principles:
	1. The Principle of Abstraction
	2. The Principle of Correspondence
	3. The Principle of Data Type Completeness

- Have to adapt to many situations

- Many situations where we don't understand how "it" works; good. Find out what we don't know

- Many don't know their actual skill and knowledge level

	>*"Jack of all trades, master of none"*

- Know how to search for the required info on internet, learn fast, and adapt it to the current environment.

- Good pen-testers become good only through practice.

## Occam's Razor

- Most simple explanation is most likely.

- Ex: Computer not working
	- Possible reasons
		- Power supply faulty
		- CPU issue
		- Motherboard fried
	
	- Process of elimination can lead to overcomplication of problem
		Ask: Why is computer not getting power?
			Forces association with the power supply.
	
	- Keep in mind to think outside the box to not limit options
		- Following cables eventually leads to finding multi-plug being off, therefore computer not receiving power.

- Must differentiate between individual components and the "big picture"
- Important to learn overall concept over individual steps
	- Ex: SQL injection's steps look difficult
		- Once the overall concept is understood, everything can fall into place and becomes easy to see if site is vulnerable to SQL injection.

- Each pen-test is different
	- Best approach usually is to work with given info
- Adapting to environments easier when overall "big picture" is understood.
- Embrace the journey, not the destination

## Talent

- Talented people are good at their **SPECIFIC** field
- Talent arises from determined confrontation with problems
	- Expands comfort zone
		- Allows to think and adapt easily

- We can influence our thought process to develop any skill we put our minds to

- Important: Talent is not born
- It is easier to learn something if you had learned something related to it before
	- Ex: A Python coder will adapt more easily to learning C++ than someone who hasn't coded before.
		- They've already learned the principles and technical skills to apply them to C++ despite being 2 very different languages.

## Learning Efficiency

- Sheer size and number of topics and concepts of info security can be overwhelming
	- Many technical and hands-on courses

- What do we already know?
- What do we not know *yet*?

- Failing is an important part of learning.
	- Builds experience
	- Helps explains how to deal with abnormal situations

- Learn what we need
- How to use it
- How to work with it

- What does it mean to be good?
	- Know what you are doing
	- Experienced
	- Repertoire
		- From associations
		- Practical experience
			- Practice needed to become competent

- Pareto Principle (80/20 rule):
	- With 20% effort, 80% effect can be achieved.
	- 80% of effort can get remaining 20% effect
	- Doesn't apply to everything, just general rule

- Overcome learning curve

- Learning Pyramid:
	- Various ways of learning can get vastly different amounts of retention and understanding of a subject.
		- Lecture gets 5% retention
		- Reading 10%
		- Audio-Visual 20%
		- Demonstration 30%
		- Discussion 50%
		- Practice 75%
		- Teaching others 90%

## Learning Types

- Passive learning
	- Just reading will only get 10% of experience
	- Watching demonstrations will get 30%

- Active learning
	- Discussing enumeration process, results can allow us to glean different view points, other sources of info, and alternative forms of thinking.
		- Will get 50% experience
	- Practice gets 75%
	- Theory can only get so far
	
	- Not all info is high-quality and helpful
	- Obtain a repertoire to decide between good and unhelpful info
		- Obtain with practice
	- Usage of info
		- Depends on motivation, focus, and goal
	
	- Good way to stay motivated is to notice progress being made.
	- Breaks are important
	- Problem-solving requires creativity
